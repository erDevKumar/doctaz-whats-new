Group video calls

A call is no longer limited to two people. Anyone on a call can bring in up to four participants in total, and everyone appears in a tile grid that rearranges itself as people join and leave. You can also invite someone who doesn’t have the app at all, by email or by phone number, and they can join as a guest.

  • Android and iPhone
  • Add people mid-call, tile layout, guest invitations

Share to Doctaz from any app

Doctaz now appears in the normal share menu across the phone. From your photo gallery, a PDF, a document, a video or a web page, you can tap Share, choose Doctaz, pick who it goes to, and send it straight into a chat without opening the app first.

  • Android and iPhone
  • Photos, videos, audio, PDFs and documents

Rate your visit, and reviews

After a consultation the patient is invited to rate it. Those ratings build a review section on each provider: an average score, a breakdown by star rating that you can tap to filter, sorting by most recent or highest rated, and the ability for a provider to reply publicly to a review. Patients can edit a review they’ve left.

  • Android and iPhone
  • Prompts after a call, on the home screen and in call history

Force update on iPhone

Android already had this; the iPhone app now matches it. When a release contains something critical, you can require users to update before they carry on, controlled remotely without shipping another version. It can be applied to everyone or to particular roles.

  • Brings iPhone to parity with Android
  • Controlled from your Firebase console, no app resubmission

What’s next

Three features, on both Android and iPhone, from one approved set of designs. Please review the designs first — development starts once you’re happy with them.

Find Care

One place to find a provider and book them. Search by name or browse by specialty, narrow the list by provider type, rating, availability and distance, and sort by whichever matters most. Each provider card shows their rating, how far away they are and whether they’re online, with a single clear action: Book Appointment.

Scheduler

The appointment engine. Patients pick a slot from the provider’s real availability and request it; providers see incoming requests and approve or decline them. Every appointment carries a clear status, so both sides always know whether something is requested, confirmed, completed or cancelled, with notifications when that changes.

Guest mode

Someone can open the app, browse providers and book an appointment without creating an account first. They confirm their phone number with a passcode as part of booking. It removes the sign-up wall that currently sits in front of every new patient.

Design freeze

Once you approve, the designs are frozen

Development runs against exactly those screens and this scope. Anything new after that — a different flow, an extra screen, another feature — becomes a change request with its own estimate, and it moves the delivery date.

This is in your interest, not mine. A change during design costs minutes. The same change in the final week means rebuilding on two platforms and resubmitting to both app stores, which is where projects like this usually lose weeks. So be as demanding as you like now.

What I need from your side

Some platform work has to land before the mobile piece that depends on it. This sits with your backend team and is not part of the four weeks.

  • Appointment booking, and approve / decline
  • Provider availability, so patients see real slots
  • Guest passcode verification that returns an app credential
  • A notifications feed, so status changes reach people

If one of these is late, that part pauses rather than gets rebuilt, and the date moves with it. Flagging it now so it isn’t a surprise later.

Already delivered, beyond the original scope

You paid for MedTalk, MedCue, the Community feed and the landing page. All of those shipped. Since then the apps have moved from version 2.3 to 2.5.1, and along the way four more features were built and released that weren’t part of that agreement.

Group video callsAdd people mid-call, tile layout, guest invitations

A call is no longer limited to two people. Anyone on a call can bring in up to four participants in total, and everyone appears in a tile grid that rearranges itself as people join and leave. You can also invite someone who doesn’t have the app at all, by email or by phone number, and they can join as a guest.

Android and iPhone
Share to DoctazPhotos, videos, audio, PDFs and documents

Doctaz now appears in the normal share menu across the phone. From your photo gallery, a PDF, a document, a video or a web page, you can tap Share, choose Doctaz, pick who it goes to, and send it straight into a chat without opening the app first.

Android and iPhone
Rate your visit, and reviewsPrompts after a call, on the home screen and in call history

After a consultation the patient is invited to rate it. Those ratings build a review section on each provider: an average score, a breakdown by star rating that you can tap to filter, sorting by most recent or highest rated, and the ability for a provider to reply publicly to a review. Patients can edit a review they’ve left.

Android and iPhone
Force update on iPhoneControlled from your Firebase console, no app resubmission

Android already had this; the iPhone app now matches it. When a release contains something critical, you can require users to update before they carry on, controlled remotely without shipping another version. It can be applied to everyone or to particular roles.

Every screen is drawn and agreed before any code is written, so you see exactly what you’re getting and can change it while changing it is free. It means acceptance is simple: the app either matches the approved screens or it doesn’t, with nothing left to interpretation at the end.

This is a significant piece of work on its own — it isn’t billed, and it isn’t counted in the four weeks.

What’s next

Three features, on both Android and iPhone, from one approved set of designs. Whenever you have time to review them, development starts as soon as you’re happy with what you see.

Find CareSearch, filter and book a provider

One place to find a provider and book them. Search by name or browse by specialty, narrow the list by provider type, rating, availability and distance, and sort by whichever matters most. Each provider card shows their rating, how far away they are and whether they’re online, with a single clear action: Book Appointment.

SchedulerAvailability, booking and approvals

The appointment engine. Patients pick a slot from the provider’s real availability and request it; providers see incoming requests and approve or decline them. Every appointment carries a clear status, so both sides always know whether something is requested, confirmed, completed or cancelled, with notifications when that changes.

Guest modeBook without an account

Someone can open the app, browse providers and book an appointment without creating an account first. They confirm their phone number with a passcode as part of booking. It removes the sign-up wall that currently sits in front of every new patient.

Tap through the prototype Comment on the designs

Both open in a browser, with no account needed. You’re welcome to leave comments directly on any screen.

Design freeze

Once you approve, the designs are frozen

Development then runs against exactly those screens and this scope. Anything new after that — a different flow, an extra screen, another feature — would come back to you as a change request with its own estimate, and it would move the delivery date.

This is really there to protect you. A change during design costs minutes, while the same change in the final week means rebuilding on two platforms and resubmitting to both app stores, which is where projects like this usually lose weeks. So please be as demanding as you like at this stage.

Next steps

  1. You review the prototype and the designs, and comment on anything you’d like changed
  2. You approve — the designs freeze and development starts
  3. Your backend team starts on the booking, availability, guest passcode and notification endpoints the apps will call
  4. Android, then iPhone, then release to both stores

That backend work sits with your team and isn’t part of the four weeks. If any of it arrives late, that part simply pauses rather than gets rebuilt, and the date moves with it.

Why this fits in 10 weeks

Three reasons, and they are all verifiable.

Two of the three features already exist

I logged into your live platform and worked through it as a patient, a doctor, a nurse, a facility and an admin. eHospital Book and Find Care are already in both apps, as older screens with working data connections. They are being redesigned and reconnected to the current forms rather than built from scratch. Only the Scheduler is genuinely new.

Nothing is being discovered during the build

I went through your platform's source and listed every screen, then opened them in a browser in each role. That came to 83 screens, which on a phone becomes about 139 — a thirteen-tab chart or a twenty-three-field form cannot be one mobile screen.

  • The booking flow verifies the phone number before a slot is chosen
  • There is a second, provider-initiated booking flow with its own calendar
  • "Approved Scheduled" is its own screen, not part of the main calendar
  • The provider chart has fourteen tables where the patient view has eight
  • An environment picker gates the whole app after login
  • Your symptom checker writes results into medical records, so the records list must render them

Each of those would have cost days if it surfaced mid-build.

iOS reuses the Android work

Same designs, same API integration, same business rules, same edge cases already solved. That is why two platforms do not cost twice one.

One decision worth understanding

Your forms live in the database, not in the website's code. Your admin panel has 51 active assessment types and can add more. That is why the mobile app fell behind: its fields were fixed inside the app, so when the web forms changed there was no way to follow.

The apps will read your form definitions from the same place the website does. Change a form on the web, and the apps follow automatically, with no app update and no resubmission to the stores.

It is the single most valuable decision in this plan, and the reason this will not happen again.

What already ships

Screenshots of your apps as they run today, taken on a real device and an iPhone with test accounts. They matter to the estimate: two of the three features already exist, so this is a redesign on top of a product that works, not a build from nothing.

The design system underneath

The screens are not drawn one by one. Colour, type and components come from your app's own token file, so what you approve here is what the code already uses.

Colour

Twenty-four named tokens, taken verbatim from DoctazTokens.kt. Brand teal, ink and surface, plus one colour per appointment status so a state is never guessed at.

Components

Buttons, inputs, checkboxes, list rows, status pills, notices and sheets are defined once and reused. That is why adding a screen is quick, and why the ninety-odd screens look like one product rather than ninety decisions.

Design freeze

⚠ Once you approve, the designs are frozen

Development runs against exactly those screens and this scope. Anything new after that, whether a different flow, an extra screen or another feature, becomes a change request with its own estimate, and it moves the delivery date.

This is in your interest, not mine. A change during design costs minutes. The same change in week seven means rebuilding on two platforms and resubmitting to both stores, which is where projects like this usually lose a month. So be as demanding as you like now.

Not included

Your admin panel holds 51 active assessment types and can add more. This proposal covers the eight patient-facing sections and the eight physician-order forms. The rest, including PHQ-9, GAD-7, C-SSRS, Braden, MMSE, MoCA, Glasgow, APGAR and the Wells scores, are not in scope — they are validated clinical instruments and each deserves proper attention. The same goes for the twenty-four clinical assessment forms inside the provider chart.

Also out: pharmacy, laboratory and diagnostics, symptom checker changes, phone-calendar integration, website changes (including the Find Provider → Find Care rename on WordPress), and hosting or staging work.
